# Menus are single-homed

## Status

accepted

## Context

The control center — the clock widget's menu — was generated by a one-shot
prompt and grew content that dedicated panels already own: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth
and DND tiles, a volume slider, and a live copy of the notification toast list
that the Notifications panel renders card-for-card identically. One action with
two homes drifts apart and doubles the maintenance, and every panel it
duplicated sits one click away on the same bar.

## Decision

A panel shows nothing another panel already owns — action or information.
Every action and every piece of menu content is single-homed: it lives in
exactly one menu. The flip side gives homeless content a home: what no menu
owns lands in the dashboard (the control center, renamed), which becomes the
clock widget's menu holding, top to bottom: date header, brightness slider,
now-playing card, month calendar.

Boundary calls this rule forced:

- The now-playing card is not a duplicate of the Music panel: the Music panel
  drives MPD only, while the card controls arbitrary MPRIS players (Firefox
  being the only MPRIS player on this system; MPD is not exposed over MPRIS).
- The toast list belongs to the Notifications panel alone. Its bar widget
  hides when empty, so the panel is reachable exactly when it has content —
  removing the copy orphans nothing.
- Quick toggles (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, DND) live in their panels; the power menu
  keeps its keybind/IPC entry, so its tile here was a second entry point, not
  a duplicate action — removed anyway, as the dashboard is not a launcher for
  other menus.
- One-shot actions that keybinds already own (the five screenshot modes) get
  no menu row: keybind muscle memory is their single home.

## Considered options

- Keeping quick toggles for one-stop access: rejected — duplication; the
  owning panels are one click away on the same bar.
- Renaming to "Calendar": rejected — brightness and the now-playing card are
  first-class blocks the name would undersell; "Dashboard" also scales to
  future content.
- Per-display brightness rows: rejected — the dashboard is instantiated per
  screen, so "the slider controls the display this panel is on" needs no rows
  and matches the per-screen model.

## Consequences

The dashboard carries brightness with display-local semantics (internal panel
via brightnessctl, ddc displays via ddcutil; the service learns an absolute
set and a per-display level cache so two dashboards no longer share one
`level`), a single now-playing card with player navigation (chevrons plus the
player's identity label, hidden while only one player exists; a manual pick
sticks while the panel is open and resets on reopen), and the calendar stays
as it is. Any future menu content checks ownership first: owned elsewhere, it
goes to the owner; owned nowhere, the dashboard is the candidate home. The
control-center naming is retired across the file, `panelName`, the IPC
function, and the Theme token.

Amendment: the dashboard later dropped both the date header and brightness at
the owner's request, leaving the now-playing card and the calendar. Brightness
is again owned by no menu — keybinds and the OSD are its only interfaces —
which this ADR permits: the single-homing rule bars duplicates, it does not
force a menu home onto every control.
